Binding and unbinding data in a single vector

Hyperdimensional computing (vector symbolic architectures) stores structured data (pairs, records, sequences) in one fixed-size vector using three operations. Everything below runs live in your browser with random vectors.
bundle (superpose a set):   s = a + b + c     s stays similar to each member
bind (pair a role with a filler):   r ⊗ f     dissimilar to both r and f, and invertible
unbind (ask "what is bound to r?"):   r-1 ⊗ s ≈ f + noise     then clean up: the nearest known vector is the answer

1. Random vectors are almost orthogonal

In high dimension, independent random vectors have cosine similarity near 0 (spread about 1/√D). That is what lets thousands of symbols coexist without confusing each other.

2. A record in one vector: bind roles to fillers, bundle, then ask

record = name ⊗ Lily  +  pet ⊗ cat  +  place ⊗ park  +  ...      query: pet-1 ⊗ record → nearest filler

3. Capacity: how many pairs fit before answers turn to noise

Every extra pair adds crosstalk to each unbinding. Accuracy of the clean-up (out of a vocabulary of 200 fillers) as the record grows, for several dimensions. Roughly, capacity grows linearly with D.

4. The link to our models: RoPE is binding with position

RoPE rotates each pair of dims of token e by angle k·θi: as a complex number, Rk e = e · exp(i k θi)  =  e ⊗ pk   (FHRR binding with a position vector pk)
our context vector   h = Σk w(k) Rk et(n-k)   is a bundle of position-bound tokens
unbinding position k:   R-k h ≈ w(k) et(n-k) + crosstalk  →  clean up against the vocabulary: "which token was k steps back?"

Left: for every distance k, the similarity of the unbound vector to the right token (green = clean-up picks the right token, red = wrong). Right: click a bar on the left to see the full clean-up ranking for that k. With RoPE frequencies many dims rotate very slowly, so position k and k±1 are nearly the same binding: nearby positions blur together (the model "knows roughly where" rather than "exactly where"), while random angles separate every position. Decaying weights make far tokens fade below the crosstalk, which is exactly the recency trade-off our kernels learn.
Takeaways for the token-kernel models
- Our banks already bind (RoPE rotation by distance) and bundle (the weighted sum); the readout is a linear clean-up against the vocabulary, which is why a name in the context votes for itself (section 2/4 in miniature).
- What they lack is explicit unbinding by a query: asking "what was the verb?" needs a learned role vector and an unbind step before the clean-up (the per-token prior and the router only re-weight the bundle).
- Capacity is the real constraint at D = 128: a bundle of ~128 tokens is far past the clean recall range of section 3, so the kernels must focus energy on few tokens (the "selective context" we saw) for anything to stay recoverable.
