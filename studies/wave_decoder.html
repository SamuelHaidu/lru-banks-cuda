Decoding tokens from a sum of sines

Every word of the sentence is a position. Click a word to make it emit: from its own position forward it sends one damped sine with its own frequency ω, phase φ, amplitude A and half-life. All emitted waves add into one signal. Pick a probe (shift-click a word or a dot in the future): the decoder sees only the last N samples there and estimates which words were behind and how far back each one is.

A peak at a word's frequency means that word is present. Peaks have width about 2π/N: two words closer than that in frequency blur together. The decoder below fits every word at once instead of reading peaks.

Decoded phasors at the probe arrow = decoded zv; circle = the truth

zv = A e−αd ei(ωd + φ): its length says how decayed the wave is (far back = short), its angle how far it has rotated since it was emitted.

Who was behind the probe, and how far back one row per word of the vocabulary (the sentence's distinct words)

Each lane is the distance axis (0 = the probe, growing to the left). Gray band: distance from the amplitude (coarse, no ambiguity). Ticks: distances allowed by the phase (precise, repeating every period 2π/ω). The decoder picks the tick inside the band (dot). Ring = the truth.

The math

Word v emitted at position p contributes, at position t ≥ p (distance t − p):
Av e−αv(t−p) cos(ωv(t−p) + φv)  =  Re[ zv e(−αv + iωv) n ],   n = t − x,   zv = Av e−αvd ei(ωvd + φv),   d = x − p
The window is linear in the unknown complex numbers zv (one per vocabulary word), so the decoder is least squares: N equations, 2V real unknowns. Then, per word:
present  ⇔  |zv| > threshold
damp = ln(Av / |zv|) / αv   (coarse)
dphase = (arg zv − φv) / ωv + m · 2π/ωv   (precise, m unknown)
d = the dphase closest to damp
Amplitude and phase are two rulers: the decay ruler is unambiguous but blurry (noise δ on |z| moves it by δ / (α|z|)), the phase ruler is sharp but periodic. Together they work like a vernier.

When it is possible (try each)

  • Each word needs its own frequency: the frequency is the word's identity.
  • N ≥ 2V samples: one scalar signal gives N equations for 2V unknowns. A real vocabulary of 10k words would need 20k samples, or many parallel channels (a D-dimensional signal gives D·N equations).
  • Frequencies at least ~2π/N apart, otherwise the fit is ill-conditioned and noise blows up.
  • The emitter must be behind the window: a wave that starts inside the window is not a damped sine over all of it, so the fit leaks. Listening in the future (dots) after the sentence is the clean case; probing inside the sentence needs "start the window after the newest emitter".
  • The same word twice merges (emit both "the"s or both "hill"s): two phasors at one frequency add into one z, decoded as one emission at an in-between distance. No decoder can split them.
  • Far words fade below the noise: |z| = A e−αd. A long half-life is remembered longer but its amplitude ruler is blurrier.
  • Distance is exact only up to the phase period: if the amplitude ruler is off by more than half a period the vernier picks the wrong tick (raise the noise: errors jump by exactly one period).
